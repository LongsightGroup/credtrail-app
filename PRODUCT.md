# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are institution administrators, badge issuers, LMS operators, and governance staff working inside higher-education credentialing workflows. They need to issue, review, verify, troubleshoot, and audit Open Badges records quickly without losing confidence in correctness or institutional control.

Secondary users are learners, external verifiers, employers, and institutional leaders who need clear proof that a credential is official, standards-based, and durable.

## Product Purpose

CredTrail is an Open Badges 3.0 and verifiable credentials platform for educational achievements. It provides multi-tenant badge management, credential issuance, public verification, learner access, LMS integration, governance controls, and SaaS plus self-host runtime profiles.

Success means the product feels trustworthy enough for institutional deployment, keeps standards compliance visible, reduces operational risk, and lets staff complete credentialing work with fast legibility and low cognitive load.

## Brand Personality

CredTrail should feel verified, official, modern, and academic. The voice is calm, precise, transparent, restrained, intelligent, and durable.

## Anti-references

CredTrail should not look like a generic SaaS template, playful edtech toy, consumer social product, crypto wallet, hype-driven AI product, neon fintech dashboard, or decorative portfolio site. Avoid over-decorated controls, gamified learner treatment, glassy panels, repeated glow effects, radial decoration, and novelty form controls where standard controls support the task.

## Design Principles

1. Trust before excitement.
2. Keep operational workflows list-first, legible, and direct.
3. Treat public records and credentials as official artifacts.
4. Use restrained institutional polish instead of decorative novelty.
5. Keep app, marketing, docs, public credentials, emails, and PDFs in one product family.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA accessibility is the baseline. Text contrast, visible focus states, semantic status labels, color-blind-safe status communication, keyboard access, and reduced-motion preferences must be preserved across product surfaces.
